Super Bowl Operations Analysis

A synthetic star-schema database (8 dimension tables, 6 fact tables) built in SQL Server and queried in T-SQL. It models a Super Bowl's event operations, including ticket sales, sponsorship revenue, gate entry and concessions, security incidents, staffing costs, and department budgets. The panels below are a Chart.js recreation of the Power BI dashboards built on top of it.

Insights

Super Bowl Operations Insights

01

Because the lower bowl sells the most tickets and food is the best-selling concession there, management should expand food stand capacity and menu options in the lower bowl specifically, to capture more revenue from that existing foot traffic.

02

Because food and alcohol sales peak from 12:00pm to 4:00pm and 6:00pm to 8:00pm, while merchandise spikes around 9:00pm, staffing should be scheduled to match those windows: heavier food and alcohol staffing in those windows, level staffing for merchandise and non-alcoholic beverages all day, with a merchandise staff bump added before 9:00pm.

03

Because gate entry volume peaks at 4:00pm, management should add extra staff and security specifically at that time to reduce guest wait times.

04

Because incident severity increases and response time slows later in the evening, security staffing should shift toward the 6:00pm onward window rather than being spread evenly across the day, targeting resources at the highest-risk period instead of just the busiest one.

05

Because security is the largest cost center (ahead of concessions and merchandise), leadership should prioritize security spend for the next efficiency and cost review, since it has the most room for savings.

06

Because the lower bowl already shows strong concession and ticket demand together, management should test bundling a concession credit into lower bowl ticket sales to capture that revenue upfront instead of relying only on in-venue upsell.
