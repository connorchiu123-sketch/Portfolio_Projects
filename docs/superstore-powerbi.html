Power BI

Superstore Power BI Analysis

A five-page Power BI report built on the same 9,994-order Sample Superstore dataset as my Excel pivot-table version — this time with DAX measures, a Homepage nav, and three drill-down pages (Product, Profit vs. Discount, and Customer Analysis). Every card, chart, table, and slicer below is rebuilt in Chart.js and vanilla JS, driven by the same 9,994 rows, so the filters actually filter — pick a region or a year and every panel on the page recalculates live, the same way the slicers do in Power BI Desktop.

Insights

Superstore Power BI Insights

01

Because profit margin holds up at 29.5% with no discount and is still positive (11.8%) at the 20% tier, but flips negative at every tier above that — down to −180% at 80% off — discounting should be capped at 20%, full stop. There's no tier above it that doesn't lose money.

02

Because 98.5% of customers (781 of 793) have placed more than one order, this is fundamentally a retention business, not an acquisition one — the Customer Analysis page's repeat-rate card exists specifically to keep that front and center.

03

Because Tables and Bookcases are the only two sub-categories running a net loss (−$17.7K and −$3.5K) while Copiers and Phones lead in profit despite lower sales than Tables, the Product Analysis page is built to catch a sub-category losing money before it shows up in the category-level total.

04

Because the five highest- and lowest-profit customers each swing the total by several thousand dollars in either direction, the Top/Bottom 10 Customers charts on the Customer Analysis page exist to flag both ends — who to protect, and who's actively costing money.
