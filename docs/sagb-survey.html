SAGB Member Engagement Survey

A ready-to-run member engagement instrument built for the Sports Analytics Group at Berkeley (SAGB), designed and offered ahead of joining as an incoming freshman. Every question is adapted from a published, validated survey methodology rather than written from scratch, so the instrument is backed by research from the start — not just a list of questions that sound reasonable. The goal is a short, credible tool SAGB could actually adopt as a recurring check-in across its three divisions (Projects/Consulting, Business, and Data Journalism).

Purpose

Why I Built This

As an incoming freshman hoping to join SAGB, I wanted to bring something more concrete to the table than a resume. This survey is a genuine attempt to give SAGB's leadership a tool they can use immediately: a short, research-backed way to check in on member engagement, cross-division communication, and program value — the kind of internal check-in most student orgs never have time to design properly. It's built to keep giving value after the conversation ends, not just serve as a one-off icebreaker.

The instrument is organized into three short sections — a screener, nine quantitative items, and four open-ended questions — each one adapted from an established, peer-reviewed or industry-standard framework and annotated with the research it draws from, so leadership can trust the "why" behind every question, not just take it on faith.

Why This Approach Works

Grounded in Validated Research, Not Guesswork

01

Core items are adapted from Gallup's Q12, the most widely cited employee-engagement instrument in the world, so questions like goal clarity, purpose, and resource access are already known to predict engagement rather than being untested guesses.

02

The "comfortable asking for help" item is adapted from Amy Edmondson's Team Psychological Safety Scale, the most widely validated instrument for measuring whether members feel safe taking interpersonal risks without fear of it being held against them.

03

Cross-division communication is measured because Wageman, Hackman & Lehman's Team Diagnostic Survey found this single condition predicts up to 80% of team effectiveness — especially relevant for an org with three structurally different divisions.

04

Goal-alignment questions are grounded in Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory, which treats goal clarity and acceptance as direct drivers of motivation and performance, not just morale.

05

The recommendation-likelihood question uses the standard eNPS (0–10) format, a single trackable number leadership can benchmark semester over semester instead of a compressed 1–5 satisfaction score.

06

The scale itself is fully labeled 1–5 rather than end-anchored, because research on Likert scale design finds fully anchored 5–7 point scales produce more reliable data without adding respondent noise.

07

Open-ended questions are capped at four, following survey-methodology guidance that open text drives fatigue and drop-off faster than closed questions — enough to surface real themes without tanking completion rates.

Structure

How the Survey Is Organized

Fifteen questions across three sections, kept short enough to complete in under five minutes.

Section# ItemsFormatWhat It's For
A. Screener 2 Multiple choice Segments results by division and tenure so leadership can see problems by team, not just org-wide
B. Quantitative (Org Health) 9 5-pt Likert / 0–10 scale Trackable numbers leadership can watch semester over semester
C. Qualitative 4 Open text The "why" behind the numbers, theme-coded by hand
Administration

How I'd Recommend Running It

01

Keep Section B anonymous. Items about psychological safety and cross-division communication only get honest answers under anonymity — standard practice for any instrument in this territory.

02

Run it once per semester. Volunteer and member-engagement research recommends a quarterly-to-biannual cadence: frequent enough to catch drift, infrequent enough to avoid survey fatigue in a small org.

03

Close the loop within two weeks. Failing to report results back and act on at least one theme is the fastest way an org kills trust in its own survey. I'd be glad to help own that readout as a member.
